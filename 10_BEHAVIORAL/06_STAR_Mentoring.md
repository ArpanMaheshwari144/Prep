# STAR — Mentored a New Joinee (codebase + DB + tools walkthrough)  [Konovo]

> Q ye cover karta: "teamwork" · "kisi ki help / mentor kiya" · "knowledge sharing" ·
> "bina bole aage aaya" · "leadership without authority" · "junior guide kiya".

## STAR (Hinglish)

```
   S: team me NEW JOINEE aaya — capable, achha kaam, par ek part pe ATAK gaya.
      wo part = jispe MAINE pehle deep kaam kiya tha (vendor-facing side).
      dikkat: codebase navigate karna, DB logic samajhna, internal tools use karna.

   T: formally mentor nahi tha. par us area ka context mere paas tha -> KHUD aage aaya unblock karne.

   A: proper SCREEN-SHARE session (sirf Slack reply nahi).
      CODEBASE walk-through + conventions ("aise nahi, ye pattern").
      DB layer: kaunsa table kya rakhta, data kaise flow karta, debug me kaise trace karo.
      internal TOOLS: kaunsa kis kaam ka, investigate kaise karte.
      PR REVIEW me follow-up: "kyun" samjhaya, sirf approve/reject nahi.
      CONFLUENCE page banaya (navigate · trace · queries · tools) -> aage kisi ko phir na atakna pade.

   R: wo khud problems solve karne laga -> us area me SELF-SUFFICIENT -> independently contribute.
      ramp-up kam, team ko baar-baar hand-hold nahi karna pada.
      LESSON: mentoring = jawab dena nahi, MENTAL MODEL dena -> agla problem khud solve kare.
```

## SPOKEN (English)

```
   "A new engineer had joined our team, sharp and ramping up well, but they got stuck on a part of the
    platform I had worked on deeply, the vendor-facing side. They were struggling to navigate the codebase,
    understand the database flow, and use our internal tools.

    I wasn't formally their mentor, but since I had the most context on that area, I stepped up to unblock
    them rather than let them stay stuck.

    I set up a proper screen-share session instead of a quick chat. I walked them through the codebase and
    our conventions, explained the database layer, which tables held what and how data flowed when
    debugging, and showed them how to investigate with our internal tools. I followed up through PR reviews,
    explaining the why, not just approving or rejecting. I also wrote it up as a Confluence page, so the next
    person joining that area wouldn't get stuck in the same place.

    After that, they solved most of those problems on their own, became self-sufficient in that area and
    started contributing independently, which cut their ramp-up time and freed the team from repeated
    hand-holding. It taught me that mentoring isn't answering the immediate question, it's passing on the
    mental model so they can solve the next one themselves."
```
